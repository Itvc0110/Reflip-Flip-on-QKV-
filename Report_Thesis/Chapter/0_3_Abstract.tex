\begin{center}
    \Large{\textbf{ABSTRACT}}\\
\end{center}
\vspace{1cm}
\sloppy

Large Language Models (LLMs) require substantial parameter memory and memory bandwidth during inference, which limits their deployment under constrained hardware and high-throughput serving conditions. Post-training weight-only quantization reduces these costs without full model retraining, but aggressive low-bit settings can introduce errors whose effects are not fully described by the isolated reconstruction error of each weight. In attention projections, signed weight errors interact with activations and may accumulate or cancel in the projected query, key, and value representations. Grouped Query Attention (GQA) adds further structure because several query heads share one key-value head, making the roles of the three projections distinct.

This thesis studies post-training, group-wise asymmetric INT4 weight-only quantization for QKV projections and presents two separate discrete correction stages. Flip selectively changes round-to-nearest decisions when the change reduces an aggregate activation-weighted projection residual, even if the isolated error of an individual weight increases. ReFlip is a subsequent query-only correction. For each query head in a GQA group, it holds the shared quantized key projection fixed and evaluates candidate changes to the quantized query projection through an unscaled scalar Q--K score surrogate. Kneedle-based sensitivity partitioning is used to identify a moderate candidate region for ReFlip; this partition narrows the search but does not provide an optimality guarantee. Statistical shrinkage, including the motivation of James--Stein estimation, informs the construction of a stable representative activation but is not used to claim a classical estimation guarantee for either correction stage.

The experimental evaluation separates local score-level evidence from model-level behavior. A standalone GQA case study compares round-to-nearest quantization, Flip, and Flip followed by ReFlip under a controlled INT4 setting, while full-model comparisons examine perplexity and selected downstream task metrics across the evaluated model and quantization configurations. The reported results provide evidence that the two correction objectives can reduce their targeted forms of quantization distortion in the evaluated settings, while the magnitude and consistency of model-level changes remain dependent on the model, baseline quantizer, and task. These findings support targeted post-quantization correction as a useful direction while also establishing clear limits: the scalar ReFlip objective is a surrogate rather than the complete attention operation, Kneedle performs candidate selection rather than global optimization, and local improvements do not guarantee universal downstream gains.
